# Strategy

## Use this when

You have an audit or a week of results, and `work/outliers.md` has proven posts in it. You need to decide what to try next. The output is `strategy.md`, one page. No proven posts yet: run `find-what-works` first.

## Steps

1. **Start from the goal** in the charter.
2. **Name the weak step.** The first of see → click → stay → follow → come back that is clearly failing. Fixing a later step does nothing if an earlier one is broken.
3. **Check the size.** If the brief says the usual is too small to read, the weak step is See, whatever else is wrong. Do not design a test that compares one kind of post with another: at that size the difference is chance. Bet on being seen. Or buy the test: put the same small spend behind each piece, so enough people see it to judge it (`paid-test`), and tell the owner that is what the money is for.
4. **List what could move it.** For fresh ideas use `find-the-angle`. Take candidates from the brief's plays to try, the channel's own plays, and what `find-what-works` showed. Typical levers:

   | Weak step | What usually moves it |
   |---|---|
   | See | Topic, format, where and when it is posted, paid spread of a post that already did well |
   | Click | Title, cover, first line |
   | Stay | The opening, pacing, length, cutting the slow part |
   | Follow | A clear reason to follow, a series, the profile page |
   | Come back | A steady rhythm, a series, answering comments |

5. **Pick one to three bets.** Prefer the ones that are cheap to make, likely to matter, and readable within two weeks.
6. **Write each bet before it starts:** the number it should move, the target, a second number that must not get worse (more clicks is no win if people leave sooner), how many posts it gets (at least 3, or one post tells you nothing), and the stop rule. A tie is a real result: write it down as one.
7. **Write the format sheet.** For each format: how it opens, the order of its parts, its length, what the picture or video looks like, how it ends. Take every line from the proven posts, and say which rows. The owner should never have to ask how long a post is or what it looks like.
8. **Plan the mix.** About 4 in 5 posts are modelled on a proven post or a play that has held. About 1 in 5 tries something untested.
9. **Check it fits.** Posts per week you can really make. Money inside the caps. Nothing the charter forbids.
10. **Write `strategy.md`.** Replace the old one.

## Checks before you call it done

- Every bet names a number, a target and a stop rule.
- The bets aim at the weak step.
- No bet needs a result the channel is too small to read.
- The format sheet is filled, and each line points at proven posts.
- A stranger could read the page in a minute and say what happens next week.

## Good and bad

- Good: "Bet 1: open every short with the question. Moves: share who stay past 3 s. Target: 45% (usual 30%). Posts: 4. Stop: under 30% after 4."
- Bad: "Improve content quality and post more consistently."
- Bad: "Compare three posts of one kind with three of another", on a channel where a post is seen by ten people.

## Known problems

- 2026-11-24: on a channel whose posts were seen by under ten people each, an agent designed a six-post comparison of two themes, with a scorecard. Nothing could have been read from it. Hence step 3.
- 2026-11-24: an owner had to ask "how long, what structure, what format?" after the strategy was written. Hence the format sheet.
