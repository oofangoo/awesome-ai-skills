# Find the angle

## Use this when

You have a subject but no reason for anyone to care, or every idea sounds like what everyone else posts.

## Steps

1. **List five things everyone in this subject says or shows.** These are the clichés. You are looking for what they leave out.
2. **For each, ask "what actually happens?"** The gap between what is said and what is lived is a tension. It may sit in the culture, in the subject itself, or in the person.
3. **Write the insight as one sentence:** "[These people] want X, but Y stands in the way, because Z."
4. **Keep it only if it passes three tests:**
   - it is true, and you can point to why
   - it is specific to these people, not to everyone
   - it makes them think "yes, but I never put it that way"
5. **Make 8 to 12 ideas from the insight,** using three different ways:
   - **change a part:** remove something expected, multiply it, or use it for a new job
   - **collide two things** that do not belong together
   - **turn it over:** think of the worst possible idea, then flip it
6. **Set aside the first three.** They are the warm-up, the ones anyone would have. Favour the later ones.
7. **Tie each idea back to the insight.** An idea that does not come from it is decoration.
8. **Swap test.** Put another channel's name on the idea. If it works just as well for them, it is generic. Make it more yours or drop it.
9. **Argue against your favourite.** Imagine it flopped six months from now: why? Fix what is likely and costly, or drop the idea.
10. **Pick one or two** and write each as a bet with the `strategy` skill.

## Checks before you call it done

- The insight sentence is written down and passes all three tests.
- The chosen idea is not from the first three.
- The chosen idea fails the swap test for other channels: it only works for this one.
- Nothing here is treated as proven. It becomes an experiment.

## Good and bad

- Good: "New managers want to be liked, but giving honest feedback feels like betrayal, because they were peers last month."
- Bad: "People want to be better leaders."
