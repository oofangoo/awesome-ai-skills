# Find what works for others

## Use this when

Before a channel's first strategy, at every monthly audit, and whenever your own numbers are too few to learn from. Other people's hits are a bigger sample than your own posts. The pieces a channel makes are modelled on what this study finds (`model-a-proven-post`).

**Do this inside the platform,** through the `research` route in `access.json`: open the accounts' own pages and read the numbers under their posts. If you cannot, stop and get a route that can (`channel-setup`, part 1). Articles about an account, and studies of what "tends to work", are not this study.

## Steps

1. **Pick 5 to 10 comparable channels.** Same platform and format, a similar audience. Prefer ones that grew recently from small, not giants. Mix sizes.
2. **Find each one's usual.** The median views of their last 10 to 20 posts. Use numbers you can see without signing in.
3. **Find the outliers.** Posts at 2× their usual or more. A big channel's normal post is not a hit; a small channel's 5× post is.
4. **Gather what you can for each outlier.** You may not be able to watch it. Collect the transcript or captions, a few frames, the title and cover, the top comments, the visible numbers and the size of the account. Say what you could not get.
5. **Write down how it is built.** The format, the length in words or seconds, the number and kind of pictures, how much text is on them, how it opens and how it ends. The strategy's format sheet is written from these facts.
6. **Take it apart in layers:** the opening, the feeling, the structure, the title and cover, the topic, why someone would share it, and how it was spread.
7. **Ask of each layer: "take this out, does it still work?"** Mark it as the driver, a support, incidental, or something you cannot separate.
8. **Rule out the easy explanations:** a huge account, luck, a one-time moment in the news, or only looking at winners. Check the reverse: do their ordinary posts lack the thing? If ordinary posts have it too, it is not the cause.
9. **Look for a pattern.** The same driver in at least 3 outliers from at least 2 channels. More is better. One hit is a sample of one.
10. **Turn the pattern into a test.** Write the mechanism applied to your own subject, as one bet with the pattern as the one thing that is different.

## Where the notes go

- **Driving:** `work/outliers.md` in your channel repo, one row per outlier in the table that page gives. A row counts only with a link and its ratio, written like `3.2×`. Setup needs at least 5. If a pattern could help others, describe it in `outbox/` without naming your channel.
- **Tending:** `research/outliers.csv` and a dated note in `research/`. A pattern worth testing becomes an `untested` play.

## Checks before you call it done

- Read-only. No liking, following, commenting or messaging while you look.
- Every outlier has its link and its ratio, not only its view count.
- Every outlier says how it is built: format, length, look.
- You took the mechanism, not the content. Never copy a title, script, image or idea whole.
- The pattern is written as something to test, not as a fact.

## Good and bad

- Good: "4 of 6 outliers across 3 channels open on an unanswered question in the first second. 1 of 10 ordinary posts does. Test it."
- Bad: "Top channels post daily, so we should post daily."
