# Hook

## Use this when

You are writing the first line of a post, the first second of a video, or the top of an ad.

## Steps

1. **Write the promise in one plain line.** What does the person get if they stay? If you cannot say it, the piece is not ready.
2. **Mine the piece before you write.** Pull out its strongest raw material: the most surprising fact, the highest stake, the most exact number, the sharpest opinion, the moment most people will recognise. A hook is found in the piece, not added to it.
3. **Read the plays.** Use the hook plays for your platform in `brain/`, and your channel's own.
4. **Write ten openings,** not one. Use different kinds:
   - a question the person already has
   - a threat, or something about to be lost
   - a number or fact that surprises
   - the middle of the action
   - two things that should not both be true
   - a direct "you"
5. **For video, write all three parts.** The first frame, the first words and the text on screen arrive together. Each must work, and they must not just repeat each other.
6. **Apply two gates. A hook that fails either is out, however clever.**
   - **True:** the piece delivers exactly what the hook promises.
   - **Pull:** a stranger would want the next line.
7. **Then score what is left,** 0 to 2 on each: leaves a gap the piece closes; is specific; fits before the platform cuts it off; sounds like the channel.
8. **Cut any that answers itself.** If the hook gives the payoff, there is no reason to stay.
9. **Cut any that needs context.** A stranger with the sound off must understand it.
10. **Keep the best two. Use one.** If the hook is this experiment's one difference, write its kind in the `variable` field.

## Checks before you call it done

- The subject is in the first line or the first frame.
- It works with no sound and no earlier post.
- The piece delivers what the hook promises.
- It does not ask for likes, shares or comments.

## Good and bad

- Good: "Your phone has 4% left and one bar. Who do you call?"
- Bad: "Hey everyone, welcome back. Today we are going to talk about…"
