# Niche research

## Use this when

A channel is being set up, or a month has passed. The job is to test the owner's idea against the outside world, and to say so plainly where the two disagree. The output is `research.md`.

**Do the research inside each platform the channel is on or is considering,** through the `research` route in `access.json`. Search there, open the accounts there, read the comments there. What the web says about a platform is a supplement. If you have no way in, stop and get one (`channel-setup`, part 1).

## Steps

1. **Write the intent as claims you can check.** "These people exist, on this platform, want this, and are not getting it." Each claim gets tested below.
2. **Is the audience there?** Look for evidence, not hope:
   - what people search for and ask, in their own words
   - the size and activity of the places they gather
   - public view counts on posts about this subject, and whether they are rising or falling
   Write the source and the date for each.
3. **Who else serves them?** Find 10 to 20 channels, of different sizes. For each: size, their usual views, how often they post, formats, how old the channel is. Use `find-what-works` for their outliers.
4. **Look at the newcomers.** Who started in the last year and grew: what did they do? Who started and stalled: what did they do? Looking only at winners misleads.
5. **Find the gap.** Read the comments and questions under the others' posts. What do people ask for that nobody gives them well? Use `find-the-angle`.
6. **What would it take?** How much, how often and how good is the work of those who are growing? Can we match that with our tools, time and money? What does one piece cost us?
7. **Check the rules.** Read the platform's plays and warnings in `brain/`. Does this idea depend on anything a platform penalises, such as mass-produced or reused content?
8. **Give the odds honestly.** One of: likely, possible, a long shot. Say why, what would change it, and what you could not find out. Never invent a number to look precise.
9. **Challenge the intent.** List every place the evidence disagrees with what the owner first said. For each, propose a change: a narrower reader, another platform or format, a different goal or date. If you found no disagreement, say what you checked.
10. **Propose checkpoints.** For 30 and 90 days: the numbers that would mean continue, change course, or stop.
11. **Write `research.md`,** one page. Then talk it through with the owner and record what was decided.

## Checks before you call it done

- Every claim has a source and a date. Other channels' numbers are the public ones.
- Read-only: no following, liking, commenting or messaging while researching.
- The page says what is unknown.
- The owner has seen where the evidence disagrees with them, in plain words.
- No other channel in this system is named or used. Its lessons arrive through `brain/`.

## Good and bad

- Good: "Long shot as first stated. 14 of 16 channels on this subject post daily; we can make two a week. Narrowing to one kind of reader leaves 3 channels, none posting weekly."
- Bad: "This is a growing niche with strong potential."
