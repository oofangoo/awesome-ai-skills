# Fact-check

## Use this when

A piece states facts, numbers, quotes or what a study found. One wrong claim costs more trust than the piece earns.

## Steps

1. **List the claims the piece stands on.** Usually three to five. If one were false, would the piece fall? Then it is on the list.
2. **For each, go to the original source.** Not the article about the study: the study. Not the post quoting the person: the person's own words.
3. **Check four things:**
   - who made the claim, and whether they would know
   - when; is it still true today?
   - that the source says this, in these words, about this group
   - that a number keeps its unit, its date and what it counts
4. **A link that opens is not proof.** Read the part that carries the claim.
5. **Log it** in the experiment's notes or `work/`: the claim, the source, its date, the link, the date you checked.
6. **When there is no source, choose one:**
   - cut the claim
   - say it as your own observation, clearly marked
   - ask it as a question
7. **Check the easy mistakes:** names, titles, dates, places, spelling of people and brands.

## Checks before you call it done

- Every claim on the list has a source you opened and read.
- Nothing rests on "it is widely known" or on a number with no origin.
- Round, unsourced figures are gone.
- The log exists, so the next agent can check without starting again.

## Good and bad

- Good: "Claim: 39% of Feed views are from sources people do not follow. Source: the platform's own quarterly report, first quarter 2026, read 23 Nov."
- Bad: "Studies show most people prefer short videos."
