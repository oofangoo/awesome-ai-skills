# Channel audit

## Use this when

You are new to a channel, or a month has passed since the last audit. The result feeds the `strategy` skill.

## Steps

1. **Read the charter.** Goal, audience, voice, limits.
2. **Check access.** Open every account and tool the charter lists. Note what works in `state.md`.
3. **List the last 20 to 30 posts.** For each: date, format, first line or title, link, and the numbers you can see.
4. **Backfill them.** Record each with `dcos backfill`, one main number per format (usually views). The channel then has a usual from day one.
5. **Walk the five steps** with numbers, where you can see them:
   - **See:** how many people are shown a post.
   - **Click:** how many open or start it.
   - **Stay:** how much they watch or read.
   - **Follow:** how many follow after.
   - **Come back:** how many see the next one.
6. **Compare the top 3 and bottom 3 posts.** Rank by shares, saves and the goal number, not by likes. What differs: format, opening, topic, length, day? Sort each kind of post into keep, refresh or drop. Try a refresh before a drop. With too few posts to rank, compare how they are built instead.
7. **Check the channel's standing with the platform.** Look for any restriction, strike, warning or "not recommended" status. A channel the platform has stopped recommending explains low reach better than any post does. If you cannot see the status, write that down and ask the owner to look.
8. **Look at the front door.** Name, picture, bio, pinned post, links. Would a stranger know in 5 seconds what this is and why to follow?
9. **Look outside.** Use `find-what-works` on 5 to 10 comparable channels. Put what you find next to this channel's posts: format, length, look, how they open.
10. **Write the audit.** One page, `work/audit-YYYY-MM-DD.md`:
   - the usual, per format
   - the first weak step, with the numbers that show it
   - one plain sentence: is it that nobody sees the posts, or that people see them and do not care? The two need different cures, and better writing cures only the second
   - three observations, each with its evidence
   - what you could not see, and why

## Checks before you call it done

- Every number has a date and says where it was read.
- A number you could not see is written `na`, not 0.
- No sentence claims a cause from one post. Say "one post", not "this works".
- The audit names one weak step, not five.

## Good and bad

- Good: "See is the weak step. The last 12 posts were shown to 40 to 90 people each, read on 3 March from the page's insights."
- Bad: "Engagement is low and content could be more engaging."
