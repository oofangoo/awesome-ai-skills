# Write a post

## Use this when

The piece is carried by words: a text post, an image with a caption, or a set of image cards.

## Steps

1. **Name one reader and one point.** Who is this for, and what will they know or feel after it that they did not before?
2. **Write a fact sheet first.** List only what your sources give you: what the thing is, who it is for, each number with where it came from, one or two odd true details, one limit. Where a line stays empty, that part of the post gets shorter. Do not fill it with invention.
3. **Write the opening** with the `hook` skill. It must earn attention honestly.
4. **Develop the idea.** Give the reason, the example or the steps. A post that only states a claim is a headline, not a post.
5. **Finish it inside the platform.** The reader gets the whole benefit without clicking away. If a link is needed, follow the platform's plays on links.
6. **Check every fact** (`fact-check` skill). Each number, quote and claim has a source you can open, and the date is still right. Mark what is fact, what is your reading of it and what is a guess. Never invent an experience, a result or a quote. Then read the last line alone against the fact sheet, and search for "only", "first", "most" and "never": each is a claim, so give it a source or cut it.
7. **Write it in the channel's voice,** in the language as its readers speak it. Do not translate word for word.
8. **Make the visual** with `make-image` if the post has one. It should carry part of the idea, not decorate it. One idea per card.
9. **Give a reason to pass it on or come back:** something useful to send to a friend, or a next part worth waiting for. Do not ask for shares.
10. **Revise in three passes,** one question each, about five minutes in all. Do not mix them, and change no fact.
    - Clear: would a stranger follow each sentence on first read? Split or cut what they would read twice.
    - Worth it: what did the reader get from each paragraph? Cut one that gives nothing. Put the answer first.
    - Specific: swap each general line for the detail or step from the fact sheet. Nothing there: cut the line. Keep the one example that makes a step usable.
11. **Package it** with the `packaging` skill: first line, crop, alt text, hashtags by the platform's plays.
12. **Get the fresh-eyes check.**

## Checks before you call it done

These eight must each be a clear yes. Write "pass", "fix" or "hold" for each in the experiment's notes.

1. A specific reader and a human point.
2. An opening that earns attention honestly.
3. The idea is developed.
4. The whole benefit is inside the post.
5. Evidence, honesty and voice.
6. A finished visual that belongs to the post.
7. A reason to share or return.
8. What was saved can be checked (see `ship-and-verify`).

## Good and bad

- Good: a post that opens on the reader's question, answers it with one worked example, and ends on the line they will repeat to someone.
- Bad: a claim, three general tips, and "What do you think? Comment below."

## Results

- Imported from one page's publishing standard. It kept errors out. It was not shown to raise views: that page's posts stayed at single-figure viewers for other reasons.
- Step 10, tried with and without on 2026-11-24: six test drafts, each revised once by the checks alone and once by the three passes. Two blind reviewers each chose the three-pass version in 5 of 6 pairs. Facts and voice held in all six. The one loss had cut a useful example, so the step now says to keep it. Test drafts, not channel posts.
